## The problem

Your prototype worked fine. Then real traffic arrived and the logs filled with
`429 Too Many Requests`. Retries made it worse, latency spiked, and the dashboard
shows you're nowhere near your "quota."

Azure OpenAI enforces **tokens-per-minute (TPM)** and **requests-per-minute (RPM)**
limits *per deployment*. A single long prompt can burn a minute's budget in one call,
and the limit is evaluated over short windows, not the whole minute.

## The solution

1. **Estimate tokens before you send.** Set `max_tokens` deliberately; the service
   reserves it against your TPM even if the reply is short.
2. **Retry with jitter, and honor `retry-after-ms`.** Fixed-interval retries
   synchronize your clients into a thundering herd.
3. **Spread load across deployments.** Put two or more deployments (in different
   regions if your data residency allows it) behind API Management with a
   load-balanced backend pool and circuit breaker.
4. **Move steady workloads to provisioned throughput** once you can predict them.

```bash
az cognitiveservices account deployment show \
  --name my-aoai --resource-group rg-ai --deployment-name gpt-4o \
  --query "sku"
```

Check the SKU capacity first: it's often lower than people assume.

## Takeaway

429s are a capacity-planning signal, not a bug. Measure tokens, back off politely,
and spread the load.
